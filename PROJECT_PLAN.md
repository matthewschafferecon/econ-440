# ECON 440: Course Development Plan

Last updated: July 24, 2026

## Project objective

Build a rigorous, student-centered Fall 2026 version of ECON 440: Money, Credit,
and Monetary Policy with two connected halves:

1. A College Fed Challenge policy lab from the beginning of the semester through
   the October 5 video deadline (and through November if the team advances).
2. An advanced monetary economics seminar after the initial submission.

The public-facing course website will organize the schedule, study material,
official data sources, research workflows, and seminar resources. Competition
drafts, student information, internal deliberations, and rehearsal recordings
will remain in a private course space.

## Fixed 2026 competition constraints

- Fall classes are expected to begin Monday, August 31, 2026.
- College Fed Challenge orientation: Monday, September 21 at 1:00 p.m. EDT.
- Team registration deadline: Wednesday, September 30 at 5:00 p.m. EDT.
- Video submission deadline: Monday, October 5 at 5:00 p.m. EDT.
- The submission is a maximum 15-minute presentation.
- The presentation must cover current conditions, a near-term forecast,
  significant risks, and a supported monetary policy recommendation.
- A team may have up to five presenting students, with additional alternates.
- Only five students may participate in the video, presentation, and Q&A.
- The presentation must not identify EMU, the adviser, or team members, including
  through names, logos, emblems, or school colors.
- Regional virtual Q&A invitations are expected October 27-30; Q&As run
  November 2-4.
- National finalists are contacted by November 6. The in-person final and awards
  event is November 20 in Washington, D.C.

## AI-use boundary

The Federal Reserve states that a presentation written in whole or in part by
AI is not original student work and may be disqualified. Therefore:

- AI may support faculty-authored course infrastructure, generic teaching
  examples, public data-source guides, assignment logistics, and website
  maintenance.
- AI will not draft, rewrite, or supply language, analysis, forecasts, policy
  recommendations, slides, scripts, or Q&A responses intended for the team's
  competition submission.
- Students must produce the competition analysis and expression themselves.
- Course materials will include a clear disclosure and a workflow for preserving
  student authorship.

## Development workstreams

### 1. Fed Challenge curriculum

- Convert the existing study plan into a dated, class-by-class production plan.
- Map every learning activity and interim deliverable to the national rubric.
- Build short primers on the Fed's mandate, operating framework, data
  interpretation, forecasting, risk analysis, policy transmission, and
  communication.
- Create student-authored briefing memo templates, source logs, chart audit
  checklists, forecast scorecards, rehearsal protocols, and peer-feedback forms.
- Design a Q&A preparation system that tests reasoning rather than memorized
  scripts.

### 2. Team organization

- Separate course enrollment from competition roles: all enrolled students
  complete course assessments, while the official five-person team and
  alternates are selected transparently.
- Define rotating research desks: inflation; labor market; real activity;
  financial conditions and credit; forecast and policy; inclusion and
  distributional conditions.
- Set decision rights for data definitions, forecast assumptions, slide
  inclusion, policy recommendation, and final editing.
- Confirm whether informal auditors qualify as "registered" students under the
  competition rules before listing them as team members or alternates.

### 3. Public course website

Proposed top-level pages:

- Home: course purpose, current phase, and next milestones
- Schedule: full semester calendar with an advancement contingency
- Fed Challenge Lab: rules, rubric map, workflow, and training resources
- Data Toolkit: authoritative sources, release calendars, series guides, and
  chart standards
- Monetary Policy Toolkit: FOMC institutions, implementation, transmission, and
  communication
- Assignments: enrolled-student deliverables and grading criteria
- Seminar: post-submission topics, readings, and discussion prompts
- About and Policies: participation, accessibility, attribution, privacy, and AI
  policy

The site should contain no student names, competition drafts, internal forecasts,
unreleased slides, private feedback, or rehearsal recordings.

### 4. Second-half seminar

Build a modular sequence that can contract if the team advances:

- Money demand, money creation, and liquidity
- The ample-reserves operating framework
- Monetary transmission through banks, markets, balance sheets, and expectations
- Policy rules, discretion, credibility, and forward guidance
- Financial stability and lender-of-last-resort policy
- Heterogeneous and distributional effects of monetary policy
- International spillovers and exchange-rate channels
- Monetary-fiscal interactions and central-bank independence

Each module should have a core reading, an empirical application, a discussion
question, and a short student-led component.

- **Tuesday, September 8:** evaluate inflation, expectations, wages,

### Submission day: Monday, October 5

- Complete a final compliance check in the morning.
- Submit well before the 5:00 p.m. EDT deadline.
- Archive the exact submitted video, slides, data vintages, and source log in the

### After submission

- Conduct a process retrospective and transition into the seminar.
- Maintain a light weekly Q&A practice block until October 30.
- If selected, shift designated class time to November 2-4 Q&A preparation.
- If selected for the national final, update data only as permitted and prepare
  for November 20.

## Recommended assessment architecture

Competition success should not determine course grades. A workable structure is:

- Individual economic briefing memos and data exercises: 25%
- Research-desk contributions and source documentation: 20%
- Fed Challenge analytical process and rehearsals: 25%
- Post-submission seminar leadership and responses: 20%
- Professional participation and peer feedback: 10%

Auditors can join the Fed Challenge workflow through October 5, but only
formally enrolled students receive grades. Competition-role selection should be
separate from grading and should recognize alternates and research contributions.
