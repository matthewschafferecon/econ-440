# ECON 440 Assessment Rubrics

Fall 2026 | Draft for syllabus adoption

Use four performance levels. For an assignment scored out of 100, multiply the
criterion weight by 1.00 for **Exemplary**, 0.85 for **Proficient**, 0.70 for
**Developing**, and 0.50 or below for **Insufficient**. The instructor may use
intermediate scores when the evidence falls between descriptions.

## Individual economic brief or empirical product

| Criterion | Weight | Exemplary | Proficient | Developing | Insufficient |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---|
| Economic reasoning | 25% | Mechanism is correct, explicit, and appropriately qualified | Reasoning is sound with minor gaps | Relevant concepts appear but links are incomplete | Central mechanism is incorrect or absent |
| Evidence and reproducibility | 25% | Authoritative sources, correct transformations, complete metadata, reproducible result | Sources and methods are mostly complete | Evidence is usable but poorly documented or partially reproducible | Claims lack suitable evidence or contain material errors |
| Interpretation | 20% | Separates observation, forecast, causation, and judgment; treats alternatives seriously | Interpretation is generally disciplined | Some claims outrun the evidence | Descriptive correlation is presented as unsupported causation |
| Policy relevance | 15% | Shows precisely how evidence changes a live policy judgment | Policy connection is clear | Connection is asserted more than demonstrated | Policy conclusion is missing or disconnected |
| Communication | 15% | Concise, well organized, and visually economical | Clear with modest editing needs | Meaning is recoverable but structure is uneven | Difficult to follow or materially incomplete |

## Fed Challenge contribution and professional practice

This score evaluates the process, not whether judges select the team.

| Criterion | Weight | Evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Reliability and deadlines | 20% | Preparedness, attendance, handoffs, and timely revision |
| Research contribution | 25% | Briefs, data work, chart notes, source records, or documented review |
| Intellectual contribution | 25% | Questions, synthesis, forecast or policy reasoning, and error detection |
| Revision and responsiveness | 15% | Use of peer and instructor feedback; willingness to correct claims |
| Team stewardship | 15% | Clear communication, equitable workload, respectful disagreement, and help across desks |

An exemplary contributor creates verifiable work that improves the team's
reasoning, meets commitments without prompting, responds constructively to
criticism, and helps other students understand the whole presentation.
Visibility on camera is not a substitute for contribution, and alternate status
does not preclude an exemplary score.

## Individual oral defense

The instructor asks each registered student follow-up, hypothetical,
macroeconomic-theory, and Federal Reserve implementation questions.

| Criterion | Weight | Exemplary standard |
|---|---:|---|
| Accuracy and command | 30% | Gives correct answers and explains mechanisms beyond memorized lines |
| Directness | 20% | Answers the question first, then supplies the minimum necessary support |
| Evidence | 20% | Uses relevant magnitudes or sources and does not invent precision |
| Conditional reasoning | 20% | States what would change the answer and handles plausible alternatives |
| Professional delivery | 10% | Remains composed, concise, candid, and collaborative |

Misstating a fact and correcting it is less serious than bluffing. A student who
cannot explain material assigned to their own desk has not demonstrated minimum
competence.

## Team presentation crosswalk to the national rubric

Use this rehearsal rubric on the Federal Reserve's five 1–10 categories:

| National category | A rehearsal score of 9–10 requires |
|---|---|
| Knowledge | Accurate current conditions, sophisticated monetary reasoning, command across desks, and no fragile memorized claims |
| Response to questions | Direct answers, poise, quick reasoning, persuasive defense, and honest treatment of uncertainty |
| Presentation | Coherent architecture, persuasive advocacy, readable and integrated visuals, confident delivery, and no script reading |
| Research and analysis | Logical and insightful conclusions, recommendations supported by relevant data, authoritative sources, and reproducible work |
| Teamwork and cooperation | A substantial role for every presenter and visible coordination in transitions and answers |

The team should record both the numeric score and the single highest-leverage
revision after each rehearsal. Do not average away a serious factual,
authorship, anonymity, or submission-compliance failure.

## Seminar analytical note

| Criterion | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Correct statement of the research question and result | 15% |
| Reconstruction of the mechanism | 25% |
| Evaluation of evidence or model discipline | 20% |
| Identification of a consequential assumption | 15% |
| Strong objection or alternative interpretation | 15% |
| Specific policy implication | 10% |

An exemplary note analyzes the argument in the student's own words. A plot
summary of the introduction or abstract is insufficient.

## Final policy research note

| Criterion | Weight | Exemplary standard |
|---|---:|---|
| Question and contribution | 10% | Narrow, important, answerable question with a clear contribution |
| Monetary mechanism | 20% | Correct causal or institutional logic with assumptions made explicit |
| Evidence or empirical exercise | 25% | Authoritative, transparent, reproducible, and appropriately interpreted |
| Alternatives and uncertainty | 15% | Serious competing explanation and meaningful robustness or sensitivity analysis |
| Policy analysis | 20% | Disciplined connection to a policy choice; positive and normative claims separated |
| Writing and documentation | 10% | Precise organization, complete citations, informative figures, and polished prose |

## Minimum-competence rule

Recommended syllabus rule: regardless of weighted average, a student must earn
at least 70% on both the individual oral defense and final policy research note
to receive a course grade above C. This prevents a strong team product from
masking weak individual command.
