# ECON 440: Money, Credit, and Monetary Policy

Fall 2026 | Syllabus blueprint

**Meeting time:** Tuesdays and Thursdays, 12:30–1:45 p.m.

This document separates decisions that can be fixed now from details that still

## Course purpose

ECON 440 is an advanced, applied seminar in monetary economics. During the first
part of the semester, registered students and short-term auditors operate as a
policy team preparing EMU's College Fed Challenge submission. After the October
5 submission deadline, registered students continue into an advanced seminar on
money, credit, monetary transmission, financial stability, and monetary-policy
institutions.

## Learning outcomes

By the end of the course, a registered student should be able to:

1. diagnose current macroeconomic conditions using authoritative, reproducible
   data;
2. explain monetary-policy implementation and transmission through banks,
   markets, households, firms, and the international economy;
3. develop a conditional forecast and distinguish a baseline from material
   risks;
4. recommend and defend a policy stance while acknowledging uncertainty and
   credible alternatives;
5. translate technical analysis into concise charts, prose, and oral answers;
6. evaluate monetary-economics research by its question, mechanism, evidence,
   assumptions, and policy relevance; and
7. collaborate responsibly while preserving individual accountability,
   authorship, and source integrity.

## Participation paths

### Registered students

Registered students participate in both halves of the course, complete all
individual and team assessments, and receive the final course grade.

### Fed Challenge auditors

Auditors participate through the submission deadline and may contribute to
research, discussion, rehearsal, and team review. They do not receive a course
grade. Their participation does not reduce the individual responsibilities of
registered students, and confidential student work remains in the private course
space.

If EMU advances, the instructor will announce how auditors may rejoin the
preparation process and how class time will be divided.

## Provisional assessment architecture

The weights below are designed to reward both the shared product and each
registered student's independent command. They can be adjusted before the
syllabus is issued.

| Component | Weight | Main evidence |
|---|---:|---|
| Individual policy briefs and data work | 20% | Desk briefs, reproducible figures, source records |
| Fed Challenge team process and contribution | 15% | Deadlines, peer collaboration, revision, documented contributions |
| Fed Challenge individual oral defense | 15% | Rehearsal responses and instructor questioning |
| Fed Challenge final submission | 15% | Coherent team product submitted by the deadline |
| Seminar analytical notes and participation | 15% | Reading notes, discussion leadership, short module products |
| Final policy research note | 20% | 2,500–3,500 word individual paper |

**Recommended safeguard:** a registered student must demonstrate minimum
competence in the individual oral defense and final research note to earn a
course grade above C, regardless of the team submission's quality.

## Evidence standards

Course analysis should:

- prefer primary public sources and document the release date, vintage, units,
  transformation, and retrieval date;
- distinguish observed data, forecasts, assumptions, and judgments;
- label every chart sufficiently for another analyst to reproduce it;
- preserve a claim-evidence ledger for important factual and causal claims;
- state uncertainty and serious competing interpretations; and
- correct errors promptly rather than silently replacing them.

## Collaboration, authorship, and AI

Collaboration is expected; untraceable authorship is not. Each registered
student maintains a contribution record and must be able to explain and defend
any material appearing in the team submission.

Generative AI may support brainstorming, editing, coding assistance, and
practice questions when the instructor permits it. It may not substitute for
reading sources, verifying facts, interpreting evidence, or producing a
student's assessed reasoning. Students must disclose material AI assistance and
remain responsible for every claim, citation, calculation, and sentence. The
full working policy appears in
[AUTHORSHIP_AND_AI_POLICY.md](fed-challenge/AUTHORSHIP_AND_AI_POLICY.md).

## Course rhythm

### Part I: College Fed Challenge laboratory

Ten production blocks move from orientation and data architecture through
economic diagnosis, forecasting, policy design, drafting, chart audit,
rehearsal, and final submission. The detailed workflow is in
[STUDY_AND_PRODUCTION_PLAN.md](fed-challenge/STUDY_AND_PRODUCTION_PLAN.md).

### Part II: Advanced seminar

Eight modular topics begin after submission. Each meeting combines a core
reading, an optional technical extension, an empirical or institutional
application, structured discussion, and a short analytical product. The
provisional sequence is in
[SEMINAR_READING_AND_ASSIGNMENT_PLAN.md](seminar/SEMINAR_READING_AND_ASSIGNMENT_PLAN.md).

### If EMU advances

Core seminar work continues, but selected extensions become optional and a
defined portion of class is reserved for competition Q&A preparation. For the
virtual regional Q&A, the submitted presentation remains fixed and no new
slides or data may be introduced. If EMU reaches the in-person national final,
the 2026 rules allow the team to update its original presentation with new
data; every revision must remain original student work.

## Recommended grading criteria

Use the same five dimensions across briefs, presentations, and the final paper:

1. **Economic reasoning:** correct mechanisms and disciplined causal claims.
2. **Evidence:** authoritative sources, appropriate transformations, and
   reproducibility.
3. **Judgment under uncertainty:** conditional reasoning, alternatives, and
   material risks.
4. **Communication:** clear structure, economical visuals, and direct answers.
5. **Professional practice:** deadlines, attribution, revision, and reliable
   collaboration.
