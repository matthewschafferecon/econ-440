# ECON 440 Student Authorship and AI Policy

## Purpose

The College Fed Challenge requires original student work. The Federal Reserve
states that a presentation written in whole or in part by artificial
intelligence is not original student-authored work and that AI-generated
presentations or responses may be disqualified.

This policy protects student authorship while allowing the instructor to use
technology for ordinary course administration and generic teaching
infrastructure.

## Competition work covered by this policy

The restriction applies to every item intended to influence or enter the
competition submission or Q&A, including:

- economic interpretation and claims;
- forecasts and assumptions;
- scenarios and risk assessments;
- monetary-policy recommendations;
- supporting arguments and counterarguments;
- slide structure, slide language, and chart selection;
- narration, transitions, notes, and scripts;
- prepared or rehearsed Q&A responses; and
- revisions of any of the above.

Students may not ask an AI system to generate, rewrite, polish, compress, expand,
translate, critique, or simulate responses for these items.

## Permitted faculty uses

The instructor may use AI to assist with:

- the public course website;
- course schedules and administrative materials;
- generic learning objectives and study guides;
- generic templates for source logs, forecasts, risks, and peer feedback;
- examples created solely for instruction and not incorporated into the team's
  submission;
- accessibility and formatting of faculty-authored course materials; and
- other administrative tasks that do not create or alter competition content.

Faculty-created AI-assisted materials do not authorize students to use AI on
their competition work.

## Permitted student uses outside the competition

The instructor may authorize AI use for a specific second-half seminar
assignment. Such authorization must be explicit and will identify:

- the allowed tool or activity;
- the allowed stage of work;
- the required disclosure;
- what evidence of the student's own reasoning must be submitted; and
- whether generated text may appear in the final assignment.

Without explicit authorization, students should assume that ordinary course
academic-integrity rules apply.

## Non-generative tools

Ordinary calculators, spreadsheet formulas, statistical software, code written
and understood by students, databases, search tools, citation managers, and
spelling or accessibility checkers are not automatically prohibited.

However, a tool becomes impermissible for competition work when it generates or
substantively rewrites analysis, language, forecasts, policy reasoning, slides,
or answers.

## Preserving authorship

The team will preserve a reasonable process record:

- dated individual and desk research briefs;
- source and transformation logs;
- forecast assumptions and revisions;
- meeting decisions and assigned owners;
- slide-version history;
- rehearsal feedback; and
- the final submitted files and data vintage.

These records support reproducibility, fair attribution, and compliance. They
are private course records and will not be posted publicly.

## When uncertain

Before using a tool, ask:

> Could this output create, alter, select, or improve something that might enter
> the competition presentation or Q&A?

If the answer may be yes, stop and ask the instructor before using the tool.
Do not paste competition drafts or unpublished team work into an AI service
while seeking permission.

## Violations

Potential violations will be handled under the course and university academic
integrity procedures. Because an AI-authored submission may also disqualify the
entire team, the instructor may remove impermissible material, restrict a
student's competition role, or withdraw the submission when necessary to
protect the team and comply with competition rules.

## Controlling authority

Current Federal Reserve competition rules and direct organizer instructions
control if they are stricter than this course policy.
