# College Fed Challenge 2026 Requirements Snapshot

Verified against the Federal Reserve Board's 2026 overview and national rubric
on July 24, 2026. The official pages control if they are subsequently revised.

## Format and eligibility

- The first round is an unlisted YouTube video containing a presentation of no
  more than 15 minutes.
- A team may contain up to five undergraduate students. Additional alternates
  are permitted.
- All team members must be registered at the same U.S. college or university in
  fall 2026.
- A school may register only one team.
- The presentation must not identify the school, students, adviser, or Fed
  district. School colors, emblems, and logos must also be excluded.
- The video is judged on content rather than production quality.

## Required presentation content

The presentation must include:

1. analysis of current economic conditions as of submission;
2. a near-term economic and financial forecast relevant to monetary policy;
3. significant risks, including possible effects on different groups when
   relevant; and
4. a monetary-policy recommendation, including supporting reasons and
   traditional or newer tools as warranted.

## Official scoring categories

Judges assign 1–10 points in each category:

1. knowledge of the Federal Reserve, current economic conditions, and monetary
   policy;
2. response to judges' questions;
3. presentation;
4. research and analysis; and
5. teamwork and cooperation.

The top rubric standard emphasizes accurate and sophisticated knowledge,
direct and persuasive answers, coherent delivery without reading a script,
logical conclusions supported by relevant data and authoritative sources, and
a substantial, coordinated role for every team member.

## Student authorship

The Federal Reserve states that a presentation written in whole or in part by
artificial intelligence is not original student-authored work. AI-generated
presentation or response content may result in disqualification. ECON 440
therefore prohibits AI from generating, rewriting, polishing, or rehearsing any
competition content. Faculty-created generic course infrastructure does not
become part of the team's submission.

submission form before the deadline.

## Advancement rules

### Virtual regional Q&A

- Judges question the team for up to 15 minutes.
- Two questions are common across teams.
- Question types include follow-ups, hypotheticals, macroeconomic theory, and
  Federal Reserve institutions or implementation.
- The submitted presentation may be displayed, but no new slides or data may
  be introduced into it.

### In-person national final

- Seven teams advance to the Washington final.
- Teams again give a 15-minute presentation and answer questions.
- Printed copies of the presentation should be available for judges.
- Unlike the regional Q&A, finalists may update the original presentation with
  new data.
- The registration form asks whether the team can attend the Washington final.

## Faculty verification points

Before registration, confirm:

- Eastern Michigan University's correct competition region;
- that each named team member and alternate is eligible and registered for fall;
- that EMU is submitting only one team;
- who will serve as primary contact;
- whether the team could attend an in-person final; and
- that no adviser is barred by the Federal Reserve employment separation rule.
