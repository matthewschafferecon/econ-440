# ECON 440 Fed Challenge Q&A Drill Bank

Fall 2026 | Generic faculty-led practice material

These prompts are instructional questions, not proposed answers or submission
language. Students must develop and defend their own responses without
AI-generated competition content.

## How to run a drill

### Rapid round

- Give a student 10 seconds to organize and 45 seconds to answer.
- Require the conclusion in the first sentence.
- A second student may add one point in 15 seconds.
- Score accuracy, directness, mechanism, evidence, and conditional reasoning.

### Deep round

- Ask one primary question and two follow-ups.
- Permit 90 seconds for the first answer and 30 seconds for each follow-up.
- Require the student to identify one fact that could overturn the answer.

### Team round

- Run a 15-minute simulation.
- Include one common question, one presentation follow-up, one hypothetical, one
  theory question, and one Fed-institution question.
- Do not preassign questions to desks.
- Debrief only after the clock stops.

## Follow-up questions about the presentation

1. Which single piece of evidence contributes most to your current-conditions
   diagnosis?
2. What evidence in your presentation is most vulnerable to revision?
3. Why did you use this inflation measure rather than the main alternative?
4. Does recent inflation momentum contain information beyond the 12-month rate?
5. What explains the gap between headline and core inflation in your analysis?
6. Which labor-market measure best distinguishes rebalancing from recession?
7. How do payroll revisions affect your view?
8. Why should we interpret tighter lending standards as supply rather than weak
   demand?
9. Which financial condition matters most for your forecast?
10. How do you reconcile strong activity with restrictive monetary policy?
11. What observation is inconsistent with your baseline?
12. What is the largest difference between your forecast and the SEP?
13. Which assumption explains that forecast difference?
14. Why is your top risk not already incorporated into the baseline?
15. What would make your policy recommendation a mistake?
16. Which group bears the largest near-term cost of your recommendation?
17. Which group bears the largest cost if the FOMC follows the alternative?
18. Why is your recommendation better than waiting one more meeting?
19. Why is your recommendation better than moving more aggressively?
20. How does the balance-sheet policy in your recommendation interact with the
    target range?

## Hypothetical questions

1. Payroll growth is much weaker for three consecutive months, but unemployment
   is unchanged. What changes?
2. Unemployment rises 0.4 percentage point because participation increases.
   What changes?
3. Core PCE inflation rises for two months because of one volatile component.
   What changes?
4. Inflation expectations rise at short horizons but remain stable at longer
   horizons. What changes?
5. Productivity growth is materially stronger than assumed. What changes?
6. Wage growth slows while productivity is unchanged. What changes?
7. Oil prices increase sharply because of a temporary supply disruption. What
   changes?
8. Long-term Treasury yields rise even though the expected policy path is
   unchanged. What changes?
9. Credit spreads widen and equity prices fall without a deterioration in hard
   data. What changes?
10. Banks tighten lending standards while loan demand also falls. What changes?
11. The dollar appreciates sharply. What changes for inflation and activity?
12. Fiscal policy becomes more expansionary than your baseline assumes. What
    changes?
13. Housing inflation falls faster than expected while market rents stabilize.
    What changes?
14. GDP is strong but gross domestic income and employment are weak. What
    changes?
15. A major data revision removes the turning point central to your argument.
    What changes?
16. The effective federal funds rate begins moving toward the top of the target
    range as reserves decline. What changes?
17. Financial markets price substantial easing despite unchanged FOMC
    communication. What changes?
18. The unemployment rate falls because labor-force participation drops. What
    changes?

## Macroeconomic-theory questions

1. What makes monetary policy neutral in the long run but powerful in the short
   run?
2. Which Phillips-curve mechanism is relevant to your forecast?
3. How should policymakers treat uncertainty about the natural rate of
   unemployment?
4. What is the neutral real interest rate, and why is it difficult to observe?
5. What does it mean for policy to be restrictive?
6. How do expectations affect the sacrifice ratio?
7. When does a Taylor-type rule improve policy analysis?
8. Why might strict adherence to a simple rule be undesirable?
9. What is the credit channel, and how is it distinct from the interest-rate
   channel?
10. How can borrower net worth amplify a monetary shock?
11. What is the information effect of an FOMC announcement?
12. Why might forward guidance move longer rates?
13. How do heterogeneous marginal propensities to consume change transmission?
14. How can monetary policy have distributional effects even when those effects
    are not an independent objective?
15. When can financial-stability concerns conflict with price stability?
16. What is time inconsistency, and why does credibility matter?
17. How does dominant-currency pricing affect international transmission?
18. Under what conditions could fiscal policy constrain monetary policy?

## Federal Reserve and implementation questions

1. Who votes on the FOMC, and how do Reserve Bank presidents participate?
2. What is the difference between the Board of Governors and the FOMC?
3. What happens operationally after the FOMC changes its target range?
4. Why does interest on reserve balances help control the federal funds rate?
5. Why can the effective rate differ from interest on reserve balances?
6. What role does the overnight reverse repo facility play?
7. What role does the Standing Repo Facility play?
8. What does quantitative tightening do to the Federal Reserve balance sheet?
9. How can Treasury General Account movements affect reserve balances?
10. What is the discount window, and why might banks avoid it?
11. How is lender-of-last-resort policy different from monetary-policy easing?
12. What is the purpose of the Summary of Economic Projections?
13. Is the median dot an FOMC commitment?
14. Why might the statement, SEP, and press conference convey different
    information?
15. What is the difference between the target range and the stance of policy?
16. Why does the Federal Reserve monitor payment systems and market functioning?
17. What legal or institutional limits constrain emergency lending?
18. What does central-bank independence mean, and what accountability accompanies
    it?

## Forecast and evidence questions

1. What is the forecast horizon, and why is it appropriate for today's decision?
2. Which policy path is embedded in your forecast?
3. Which variable has the widest uncertainty band and why?
4. How were desk forecasts reconciled into one baseline?
5. Which external benchmark is most informative?
6. Why does your forecast differ from that benchmark?
7. Which forecast error would matter most for welfare?
8. How do data revisions affect confidence in real-time policy?
9. Which leading indicator has proved most reliable for your horizon?
10. Are your probabilities mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive?
11. What signpost would move a risk into the baseline?
12. How do you prevent double-counting correlated risks?
13. What is the causal interpretation of the strongest correlation in your
    presentation?
14. Which chart could be removed with the least loss?
15. What evidence would falsify your mechanism?

## Policy-choice questions

1. State your recommendation in one sentence.
2. Why this size of move rather than the nearest alternatives?
3. Why act now rather than wait?
4. Is the recommendation based on the baseline, risk management, or both?
5. How does your recommendation balance both sides of the mandate?
6. What does your recommendation assume about neutral policy?
7. What financial conditions do you expect after the decision?
8. Would an unchanged target range still constitute a change in stance?
9. How should the FOMC communicate conditionality without increasing
   uncertainty?
10. What would trigger the next move?
11. What would justify reversing the move?
12. How does balance-sheet policy support or complicate the recommendation?
13. What is the strongest argument against your recommendation?
14. Which error is more costly under your baseline?
15. How would you explain the recommendation to a household facing high prices?
16. How would you explain it to a newly unemployed worker?
17. Could a supervisory or liquidity tool address the risk more directly than
    the policy rate?
18. What would your recommendation be if the FOMC had perfect credibility?

## Teamwork and correction drills

1. The first respondent makes a factual error. Practice a concise, respectful
   correction by another student.
2. Two students begin answering simultaneously. Practice a clean handoff.
3. The question spans two desks. Practice one integrated answer rather than two
   miniature speeches.
4. A judge rejects the premise of the team's forecast. Defend the mechanism
   without becoming defensive.
5. A judge asks for a number the team does not know. State what is known, avoid
   invention, and explain where the answer would come from.
6. A judge asks an ambiguous question. Clarify briefly without evading it.
7. A student gives a long answer. Practice an interruption that sharpens rather
   than contradicts the response.
8. A follow-up reveals genuine uncertainty. Practice acknowledging it and
   returning to the decision-relevant point.

## Debrief scorecard

For each answer, record 0, 1, or 2:

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Directness | Did not answer | Answer emerged late | Answered immediately |
| Accuracy | Material error | Broadly right, imprecise | Correct and precise |
| Mechanism | Missing | Implied | Explicit causal chain |
| Evidence | None or invented | Relevant but vague | Decisive and appropriately qualified |
| Conditionality | Absolute or evasive | Mentions uncertainty | States what would change the view |
| Teamwork | Collision or contradiction | Neutral handoff | Coordinated addition or correction |

After scoring, identify only:

1. the most important correction;
2. one stronger piece of evidence; and
3. the next follow-up question.
