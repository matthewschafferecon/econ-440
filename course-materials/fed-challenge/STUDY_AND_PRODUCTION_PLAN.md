# ECON 440 College Fed Challenge Study and Production Plan

Fall 2026 | Faculty-facing master plan

## Purpose

This plan turns the first five weeks of ECON 440 into an intensive monetary-policy
lab. It is designed for a mixed group of five full-semester students and several
first-half auditors, while preserving a clear distinction between:

- learning activities completed by everyone;
- graded work completed by enrolled students;
- research contributions made by the wider group; and
- the five students who may appear in the College Fed Challenge video and Q&A.

The team owns every competition claim, forecast, recommendation, slide, and
spoken word. Faculty-provided materials structure the learning and production
process but do not supply competition content.

## What success means

By October 5, students should be able to:

1. Explain the Federal Reserve's mandate, structure, operating framework, and
   monetary-policy transmission mechanisms.
2. Evaluate the current state of inflation, employment, real activity, and
   financial conditions using authoritative data.
3. Construct and defend a transparent near-term forecast.
4. Identify material upside and downside risks and explain which evidence would
   make each risk more or less likely.
5. Formulate and defend a monetary-policy recommendation using a coherent loss
   function or decision framework.
6. Communicate the argument logically within 15 minutes.
7. Respond concisely and analytically to follow-up, hypothetical, theory, and
   Federal Reserve questions.
8. Demonstrate substantial contributions and coordination across all five
   presenters.

These outcomes map directly to the national score categories: knowledge,
responses to questions, presentation, research and analysis, and teamwork.

## Non-negotiable competition requirements

- Registration: September 30, 2026, 5:00 p.m. EDT.
- Video submission: October 5, 2026, 5:00 p.m. EDT.
- Maximum presentation length: 15 minutes.
- Required content: current conditions, near-term forecast, significant risks,
  and a supported monetary-policy recommendation.
- Maximum presenters: five; alternates are permitted but may not appear in the
  video, presentation, or Q&A.
- Anonymity: no school, adviser, or student identification, including names,
  colors, emblems, or logos.
- Student authorship: no AI-generated competition content.

## Research organization

### Research desks

Every student begins on one primary desk and one secondary desk. Membership can
rotate after the baseline briefing.

1. **Prices and inflation**
   - CPI and PCE, headline and core
   - goods, housing, and non-housing services
   - wages, productivity, expectations, and supply-side pressures

2. **Labor market and inclusion**
   - payrolls, unemployment, participation, hours, and earnings
   - vacancies, quits, layoffs, claims, and labor-market flows
   - outcomes by demographic group, education, geography, or disability status

3. **Real activity and demand**
   - GDP and GDI, consumption, investment, government, and trade
   - industrial production, surveys, housing, and business formation
   - nowcasts and near-term growth indicators

4. **Credit and financial conditions**
   - Treasury yields, term and credit spreads, equity prices, and the dollar
   - bank lending, consumer credit, delinquencies, and lending standards
   - transmission from policy rates to households and firms

5. **Forecast, risks, and policy**
   - common forecast assumptions and forecast table
   - alternative scenarios and signposts
   - policy benchmarks, implementation tools, and communication

### Cross-desk roles

- **Source steward:** checks provenance, units, transformations, and vintages.
- **Forecast coordinator:** maintains one forecast table and assumptions log.
- **Devil's advocate:** records the strongest counterargument to each conclusion.
- **Integrator:** tracks dependencies and unresolved contradictions across desks.
- **Presentation coordinator:** manages time allocation and visual consistency
  without rewriting students' analysis.

Roles rotate so that responsibility does not concentrate in one or two students.

## Recurring student-owned artifacts

### One-page economic brief

Each desk submits a concise brief containing:

- one-sentence bottom line;
- three claims, each paired with evidence;
- two charts or tables;
- one disagreement or measurement caveat;
- one near-term forecast implication;
- one policy implication;
- one question the desk cannot yet answer; and
- a complete source and transformation log.

### Claim-evidence ledger

For every proposed presentation claim, record:

- claim in the student's own words;
- measure and source;
- observation window and data vintage;
- transformation and units;
- why the evidence supports the claim;
- strongest alternative interpretation;
- confidence level;
- owner; and
- date last checked.

### Forecast table

At minimum, track:

- real GDP growth;
- unemployment rate;
- payroll growth or another labor-market flow measure;
- headline and core PCE inflation;
- an agreed financial-conditions measure; and
- the federal funds rate path or policy assumption.

For each variable include the historical endpoint, forecast horizon, central
estimate, plausible range, conditioning assumptions, and comparison benchmarks
such as the SEP, professional forecasts, or market pricing.

### Risk register

Each risk entry includes:

- transmission chain;
- variables most exposed;
- probability and impact stated separately;
- leading indicators;
- conditions that would invalidate the risk;
- policy relevance; and
- distributional or sectoral effects where material.

### Decision matrix

Students compare the preferred policy with at least two alternatives using:

- inflation outlook;
- employment outlook;
- balance of risks;
- lags and uncertainty;
- financial-stability considerations;
- effects on different groups, borrowers, or savers;
- implementation considerations; and
- communication risks.

The matrix organizes reasoning; it does not mechanically determine the decision.

## Ten-session production sequence

The course meets Tuesdays and Thursdays from 12:30–1:45 p.m. The ten sessions
below run from September 1 through October 1, leaving October 2–5 for the final
employment-release review, controlled re-recording if necessary, and submission.

### Session 1 — Tuesday, September 1, 12:30–1:45

**Mandate, rules, and baseline diagnostic**

**Preparation**

- Read the 2026 overview, national rubric, and course authorship policy.
- Review the Fed's structure, dual mandate, and FOMC composition.

**In class**

- Compare the work of an FOMC briefing with the four required presentation
  components.
- Complete an individual diagnostic on data interpretation, implementation, and
  policy transmission.
- Establish research desks and shared definitions.

**Exit product**

- Personal learning plan.
- Desk charter with scope, sources, and first assignments.

**Quality gate**

- Every participant can state the four required components and five score
  categories without notes.

### Session 2 — Thursday, September 3, 12:30–1:45

**Monetary-policy implementation and transmission**

**Preparation**

- Study the ample-reserves framework, interest on reserve balances, the
  overnight reverse-repurchase facility, the standing repo facility, open
  market operations, and balance-sheet policy.

**In class**

- Trace a policy-rate change through money markets, broader financial
  conditions, spending, employment, and inflation.
- Separate the policy stance from the mechanics used to implement it.
- Identify transmission lags and channels that may be unusually strong or weak.

**Exit product**

- A student-drawn transmission map.
- A two-minute oral explanation of implementation, delivered without jargon.

**Quality gate**

- Students can distinguish the target range, effective federal funds rate,
  administered rates, and balance-sheet tools.

### Session 3 — Tuesday, September 8, 12:30–1:45

**Inflation and price stability**

**Preparation**

- Update CPI and PCE measures and document the latest data vintages.
- Decompose inflation across goods, housing, and other services.

**In class**

- Compare 1-, 3-, 6-, and 12-month annualized rates where appropriate.
- Distinguish relative-price shocks from persistent inflation.
- Evaluate wage, productivity, expectations, and supply-side evidence.

**Exit product**

- Prices desk brief and claim-evidence ledger.
- Two candidate inflation charts that pass the chart audit.

**Quality gate**

- No student describes disinflation as falling prices.
- Every rate states the index, component, period, seasonal adjustment, and units.

### Session 4 — Thursday, September 10, 12:30–1:45

**Labor markets, activity, and inclusion**

**Preparation**

- Update payrolls, unemployment, participation, hours, earnings, vacancies,
  claims, GDP/GDI, consumption, investment, production, and housing indicators.

**In class**

- Reconcile establishment and household survey signals.
- Distinguish levels, rates of change, and gaps from normal.
- Evaluate whether demand is above, near, or below sustainable capacity.
- Identify one distributional dimension that is analytically relevant rather
  than decorative.

**Exit product**

- Labor and real-activity desk briefs.
- A common list of genuine cross-desk disagreements.

**Quality gate**

- Claims acknowledge revisions, sampling uncertainty, and conflicting
  indicators where relevant.

### Session 5 — Tuesday, September 15, 12:30–1:45

**Credit and financial conditions**

**Preparation**

- Update policy rates, Treasury yields, mortgage and corporate borrowing costs,
  spreads, bank credit, lending standards, consumer credit, and delinquencies.

**In class**

- Separate the stance of monetary policy from the overall tightness of
  financial conditions.
- Trace conditions through banks, capital markets, household balance sheets,
  and firms.
- Identify possible financial-stability or credit-supply nonlinearities.

**Exit product**

- Financial-conditions brief.
- One transmission case study connecting a market or credit measure to spending.

**Quality gate**

- Students do not infer causation from a single asset-price movement.

### Session 6 — Thursday, September 17, 12:30–1:45

**FOMC synthesis, forecast design, and baseline forecast**

**Preparation**

- Study the latest SEP and at least two independent forecast benchmarks.
- Bring individual forecasts with assumptions, not just point estimates.

**In class**

- Set a common forecast horizon and information cutoff.
- Reconcile desk forecasts into one internally consistent baseline.
- Identify which indicators lead, coincide with, or lag the economy.
- Record disagreements and the evidence needed to resolve them.

**Exit product**

- Forecast table version 1.
- Assumptions log.
- Forecast comparison chart.

**Quality gate**

- The forecast is internally coherent and distinguishable from a description of
  current conditions.

### Session 7 — Tuesday, September 22, 12:30–1:45

**Risks, scenarios, and decision framework**

**Preparation**

- Each student proposes one upside and one downside scenario.
- Each proposal must include a transmission chain and observable signposts.

**In class**

- Rank risks by probability and impact separately.
- Stress-test the baseline against changes in inflation persistence, labor demand,
  productivity, energy or supply conditions, fiscal conditions, and credit.
- Agree on decision criteria before debating the policy instrument.

**Exit product**

- Risk register version 1.
- Two alternative scenarios.
- Decision matrix version 1.

**Quality gate**

- Risks are mechanisms, not lists of news topics.
- The decision framework can explain what would change the team's view.

### Session 8 — Thursday, September 24, 12:30–1:45

**Presentation architecture and oral defense**

This session occurs after the September 15–16 FOMC meeting, its Summary of
Economic Projections, and the September 21 College Fed Challenge orientation.

**Preparation**

- Read the statement, implementation note, projection materials, and Chair's
  press-conference transcript.
- Compare the new SEP with the team's pre-meeting forecast.

**In class**

- Identify what changed in the FOMC's outlook, risk assessment, and reaction
  function.
- Revisit the baseline and policy alternatives without simply imitating the
  Committee's decision.
- Students design the complete presentation logic and time budget.

**Exit product**

- Student-owned presentation architecture.
- Forecast table version 2.
- Documented reasons for retaining or revising prior conclusions.

**Quality gate**

- Each required competition component has a clear analytical purpose and owner.
- The argument can be summarized as evidence -> outlook -> risks -> decision.

### Session 9 — Tuesday, September 29, 12:30–1:45

**Full rehearsal, adversarial review, and registration readiness**

The September 30 GDP/PCE releases occur the next morning. Students should
preassign the release review and criteria for reopening any claim.

**Preparation**

- Refresh every included data series and verify the information cutoff.
- Complete a timed run without faculty interruption.

**In class**

- Score the run using the national rubric.
- Conduct four Q&A rounds: follow-up, hypothetical, theory, and Fed mechanics.
- Test whether each member can answer outside their primary desk.
- Decide which changes are analytically necessary and freeze cosmetic changes.

**Exit product**

- Rubric score with evidence for every rating.
- Prioritized revision log.
- Updated claim-evidence ledger and source list.

**Quality gate**

- The run finishes with at least 30 seconds of margin.
- Every chart has an owner and passes the chart audit.
- Every student gives direct answers before qualification or elaboration.

### Session 10 — Thursday, October 1, 12:30–1:45

**Recording and submission control**

**Preparation**

- Assign the October 2 Employment Situation to a release team. Incorporate it
  only if students judge it material, can do so without destabilizing the
  presentation, and document the revision.
- Freeze analytical content and complete the anonymity checklist.

**In class / production window**

- Record an early final candidate.
- Review only for technical integrity, accuracy, timing, anonymity, and
  disqualifying issues.
- Re-record only if a defined acceptance criterion fails.
- Upload as an unlisted YouTube video and verify playback from a signed-out or
  private browser session.

**Exit product**

- Submitted link.
- Private archive of the exact submitted video, slides, source log, data
  vintages, and compliance checklist.

**Quality gate**

- Submission is complete well before 5:00 p.m. EDT on October 5.

## Q&A preparation system

### Four-question rotation

Every rehearsal includes one question from each official category:

1. Follow-up on the team's evidence or recommendation.
2. Hypothetical change to the outlook.
3. Macroeconomic theory.
4. Federal Reserve institutions or implementation.

### Answer discipline

Students practice a compact structure:

1. Answer the question directly.
2. Give the core mechanism.
3. Cite the most relevant evidence.
4. State the policy implication or condition under which the answer changes.

This is a reasoning aid, not a memorized script.

### Stress-test rules

- The first respondent is selected randomly.
- A second student may add only a genuinely distinct point.
- The questioner may change one assumption mid-answer.
- Students must acknowledge uncertainty without becoming evasive.
- Every answer is debriefed for accuracy, directness, persuasion, and teamwork.

## Chart audit

Before a visual can enter the presentation, confirm:

- the source is authoritative and linked;
- the series name and identifier are recorded;
- the latest observation and vintage are correct;
- units, frequency, seasonal adjustment, and transformation are explicit;
- annualized and year-over-year rates are not confused;
- missing values and revisions are understood;
- axes are honest and labels are readable;
- the visual makes one analytical point;
- the spoken claim is supported by the displayed evidence;
- the chart does not expose school identity; and
- another student can reproduce it.

## Final submission checklist

### Content

- [ ] Current conditions are accurate as of the chosen cutoff.
- [ ] The forecast is explicit, near-term, and relevant to policy.
- [ ] Risks include mechanisms, signposts, and policy relevance.
- [ ] The recommendation covers appropriate instruments and supporting reasons.
- [ ] Claims and recommendations are supported by relevant data.
- [ ] Sources are authoritative and documented.

### Presentation

- [ ] Total duration is no more than 15 minutes.
- [ ] Organization is logical and persuasive.
- [ ] Each of the five presenters has a substantial, integral role.
- [ ] Audio and visuals are clear.
- [ ] Students do not depend on reading a script.
- [ ] The team can defend its assumptions under pressure.

### Compliance and technical

- [ ] No names, school name, school colors, emblems, logos, or other identifiers.
- [ ] All competition content is original student work.
- [ ] Video is uploaded to YouTube as unlisted.
- [ ] Title follows the required `College Fed Challenge 2026 Team #...` format.
- [ ] Video plays from beginning to end and is viewable through the submitted URL.
- [ ] Submission form is completed before October 5 at 5:00 p.m. EDT.
- [ ] Confirmation is saved in the private course archive.

## Post-submission contingency

### If EMU is not selected

- Complete a process retrospective.
- Compare the team's October forecast with subsequent releases.
- Transition fully to the advanced monetary-economics seminar.

### If EMU is selected for the November 2-4 regional Q&A

- Keep the submitted slides and data fixed, as required.
- Run short, high-frequency Q&A drills.
- Expand cross-desk knowledge and practice hypothetical shocks.
- Verify virtual-platform logistics and backup arrangements.

### If EMU reaches the November 20 national final

- Update data only as competition rules permit.
- Re-run the complete claim and chart audits.
- Prepare travel, printed materials, and room-procedure checklists.
- Preserve the students' original authorship in every revision.
