# ECON 440 Advanced Monetary Economics Seminar

Fall 2026 | Provisional reading and assignment plan

## Design

The post-submission seminar has eight modules. Each includes an accessible core
reading, a technical extension, an empirical or institutional application, a
discussion question, and a short student product.

## Dated meeting sequence

The course meets Tuesdays and Thursdays from 12:30–1:45 p.m. Each module uses
two meetings: the first reconstructs the mechanism and argument; the second
evaluates evidence, applies the idea, and develops the student product.

| October 8 and 13 | 1. Money demand, money creation, and liquidity |
| October 15 and 27 | 2. The ample-reserves operating framework |
| October 29 and November 3 | 3. Monetary transmission and identification |
| November 5 and 10 | 4. Rules, discretion, credibility, and communication |
| November 12 and 17 | 5. Financial stability and lender of last resort |

| December 1 and 3 | 7. International spillovers and the global dollar |

There is no class October 20 or 22 during Fall Break or November 26 during
Thanksgiving recess. The November 3 meeting becomes regional Q&A preparation if
EMU advances, with the module application reassigned or compressed.

For every core reading, students submit a one-page analytical note:

1. What question is the reading designed to resolve?
2. What mechanism or institution generates the central result?
3. What evidence or model discipline supports the claim?
4. Which assumption is doing the most work?
5. What is the strongest objection or limitation?
6. What would a policymaker do differently if the result is correct?

The note is an analysis of the argument, not a summary of the abstract.

## Module 1: Money demand, money creation, and liquidity

**Core:** Michael McLeay, Amar Radia, and Ryland Thomas,
["Money Creation in the Modern
Economy"](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/quarterly-bulletin/2014/q1/money-creation-in-the-modern-economy),
Bank of England Quarterly Bulletin, 2014 Q1.

**Extension:** Ben Bernanke and Mark Gertler,
["Inside the Black Box: The Credit Channel of Monetary Policy
Transmission"](https://pubs.aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/jep.9.4.27), Journal of
Economic Perspectives 9(4), 1995.

**Application:** Compare deposits, bank credit, reserve balances, and a broad
monetary aggregate during a conventional tightening, the 2008 crisis, the 2020
response, and the latest balance-sheet normalization. Separate accounting
relationships from behavioral causation.

**Discussion:** If banks create deposits when they lend, in what sense can
monetary policy constrain money and credit creation?

**Product:** A two-page balance-sheet walkthrough showing a bank loan, deposit
creation, interbank settlement, and the roles of reserves and capital.

## Module 2: The ample-reserves operating framework

**Core:** Jane Ihrig, Zeynep Senyuz, and Gretchen Weinbach,
["Implementing Monetary Policy in an 'Ample-Reserves' Regime: The
Basics"](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/implementing-monetary-policy-in-an-ample-reserves-regime-the-basics-note-1-of-3-20200701.html),
FEDS Notes, 2020.

**Extensions:**

- Sriya Anbil, Sebastian Infante, and Zeynep Senyuz,
  ["A Tale of Demand and Supply for Central Bank
  Reserves"](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/a-tale-of-demand-and-supply-for-central-bank-reserves.htm),
  FEDS 2026-028.
- Burcu Duygan-Bump and R. Jay Kahn,
  ["The Central Bank Balance-Sheet
  Trilemma"](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/notes/feds-notes/the-central-bank-balance-sheet-trilemma-20260114.html),
  FEDS Notes, 2026.

**Application:** Track the effective federal funds rate relative to interest on
reserve balances, the ON RRP rate, and other money-market rates. Relate spreads
to reserves, ON RRP usage, the Treasury General Account, and implementation
notes.

**Discussion:** How can reserves be ample but not abundant? Which prices and
quantities would reveal approaching scarcity?

**Product:** A memo explaining how a 25-basis-point FOMC decision becomes an
effective change in overnight rates without daily reserve-supply fine-tuning.

## Module 3: Monetary transmission and identification

**Core:** Bernanke and Gertler,
["Inside the Black Box"](https://pubs.aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/jep.9.4.27).

**Extension:** Emi Nakamura and Jón Steinsson,
["High-Frequency Identification of Monetary Non-Neutrality: The Information
Effect"](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/133/3/1283/4828341),
Quarterly Journal of Economics 133(3), 2018.

**Application:** Construct an event study around one FOMC announcement using
short rates, longer yields, equity prices, the dollar, and inflation
compensation. Compare the market response with the statement and press
conference.

**Discussion:** When equity prices rise after a rate increase, does that imply
tightening is expansionary, or that the announcement contained information
about the outlook?

**Product:** An identification brief stating the causal question, identifying
assumption, threats, and defensible interpretation.

## Module 4: Rules, discretion, credibility, and communication

**Core:**

- Ben Bernanke, ["The Taylor Rule: A Benchmark for Monetary
  Policy?"](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/the-taylor-rule-a-benchmark-for-monetary-policy/),
  Brookings, 2015.
- Federal Reserve Board, ["Principles for the Conduct of Monetary
  Policy"](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/principles-for-the-conduct-of-monetary-policy.htm).

**Extension:** Richard Clarida, Jordi Galí, and Mark Gertler,
["The Science of Monetary Policy: A New Keynesian
Perspective"](https://www.nber.org/papers/w7147), NBER Working Paper 7147.

**Application:** Compare Taylor-rule prescriptions using alternative inflation
measures, gap estimates, neutral rates, coefficients, and inertia. Explain
differences from the actual policy path without treating a rule as an answer key.

**Discussion:** Which forms of discretion improve policy under uncertainty, and
which weaken credibility or accountability?

**Product:** A rule-versus-discretion memo that presents the strongest argument
on both sides before reaching a qualified conclusion.

## Module 5: Financial stability and lender of last resort

**Core:** Dietrich Domanski, Richhild Moessner, and William Nelson,
["Central Banks as Lender of Last Resort: Experiences during the 2007-2010
Crisis and Lessons for the
Future"](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/central-banks-as-lender-of-last-resort-experiences-during-the-2007-2010-crisis-and-lessons-for-the-future.htm),
FEDS 2014-110.

**Extension:** Federal Reserve Board,
["Why Do We Need Both Liquidity Regulations and a Lender of Last
Resort?"](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/why-do-we-need-both-liquidity-regulations-and-a-lender-of-last-resort.htm),
FEDS 2015-011.

**Application:** Use the Fed's
[facility archive](https://www.federalreserve.gov/funding-credit-liquidity-and-loan-facilities.htm)
to analyze one 2008 or 2020 facility: market failure, authority,
counterparties, collateral, pricing, risk protection, announcement effects, and
exit.

**Discussion:** How should a central bank distinguish illiquidity from
insolvency when the distinction is least observable and time is most scarce?

**Product:** A facility-design memo with safeguards and sections on moral hazard
and distributional consequences.

## Module 6: Heterogeneity and distributional transmission

**Core:** Greg Kaplan and Giovanni Violante,
["Microeconomic Heterogeneity and Macroeconomic
Shocks"](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Fjep.32.3.167),
Journal of Economic Perspectives 32(3), 2018.

**Extension:** Adrien Auclert,
["Monetary Policy and the Redistribution
Channel"](https://www.aeaweb.org/articles?id=10.1257%2Faer.20160137),
American Economic Review 109(6), 2019.

**Application:** Use the Distributional Financial Accounts, Survey of Consumer
Finances, and labor data to compare exposure through nominal positions,
adjustable rates, labor income, housing and asset prices, and marginal
propensities to consume.

**Discussion:** Should distributional consequences enter the policy objective,
the transmission analysis, or both?

**Product:** A household-exposure matrix tracing direct and indirect effects of
tightening across four stylized balance sheets.

## Module 7: International spillovers and the global dollar

**Core:** Gita Gopinath and coauthors,
["Dominant Currency Paradigm"](https://pubs.aeaweb.org/doi/10.1257/aer.20171201),
American Economic Review 110(3), 2020.

**Extension:** Hélène Rey,
["International Channels of Transmission of Monetary Policy and the Mundellian
Trilemma"](https://www.nber.org/papers/w21852), NBER Working Paper 21852.

**Counterpoint:** Geert Bekaert and Arnaud Mehl,
["On the Global Financial Market Integration 'Swoosh' and the
Trilemma"](https://www.nber.org/papers/w23124), NBER Working Paper 23124.

**Application:** Trace one U.S. policy surprise through the broad dollar,
foreign yields, financial conditions, capital-flow measures, and trade prices.
Separate exchange-rate, dollar-funding, and risk-appetite channels.

**Discussion:** Does a floating exchange rate preserve autonomy when global
intermediaries, dollar invoicing, and capital flows transmit U.S. policy?

**Product:** A two-country spillover map and an adjudication of the
trilemma-versus-dilemma debate.

## Module 8: Monetary-fiscal interactions and central-bank independence

**Core:** Edward Nelson,
["The Practice of U.S. Monetary Policy Independence from Martin to
Greenspan"](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/the-practice-of-us-monetary-policy-independence-from-martin-to-greenspan.htm),
FEDS 2026-014.

**Extension:** International Monetary Fund,
["Monetary Policy Frameworks: An Index and New
Evidence"](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/001/2022/022/article-A001-en.xml),
IMF Working Paper 2022/022.

**Application:** Analyze the mandate, operational independence, congressional
oversight, Treasury debt management, the Fed balance sheet, interest on
reserves, remittances, and fiscal conditions that complicate stabilization.

**Discussion:** What type of independence does monetary policy require, and what
accountability makes it legitimate rather than technocratic insulation?

**Product:** A governance memo distinguishing goal, instrument, personnel,
financial, and communication independence.

## Culminating seminar assessment

Students produce a 2,500-3,500 word policy research note that:

- poses a narrow monetary-economics question;
- explains a mechanism;
- uses authoritative evidence or a transparent empirical exercise;
- states identifying or modeling assumptions;
- considers a serious alternative interpretation;
- connects the result to a live policy decision; and
- distinguishes positive analysis from normative judgment.
