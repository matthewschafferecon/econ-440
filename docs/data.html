Data sources

Recording your sources.

A number of charts and figures will appear in the presentation. Be sure to record where the data comes from (publisher, series number, URL, etc.) and make sure you’re using the most recent release.

Federal Reserve and policy

  • FRED Federal Reserve Economic Data from the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis.
  • FOMC calendars and meeting materials Statements, implementation notes, minutes, press conferences, and projections.
  • Monetary Policy Report Semiannual synthesis of policy and economic conditions.
  • Beige Book Qualitative regional evidence collected before FOMC meetings.
  • Policy tools Official descriptions of administered rates, operations, and facilities.
  • Federal Reserve balance sheet H.4.1 levels, composition, and recent developments.

Prices and inflation

  • BLS Consumer Price Index Headline, core, shelter, goods, services, and detailed components.
  • BEA PCE price index The FOMC's primary inflation measure; released with Personal Income and Outlays.
  • New York Fed Survey of Consumer Expectations Household expectations at multiple horizons and across the distribution.
  • Cleveland Fed inflation nowcasting Model estimates before CPI and PCE releases; label clearly as nowcasts.
  • Federal Reserve H.15 Nominal and inflation-indexed Treasury yields used to study inflation compensation.

Labor market

  • Employment Situation Payrolls, unemployment, participation, hours, and wages from two surveys.
  • Current Employment Statistics Industry payrolls, hours, earnings, diffusion, and revisions.
  • Current Population Survey Employment status, participation, and demographic detail.
  • JOLTS Vacancies, hires, quits, layoffs, and separations.
  • Employment Cost Index Composition-controlled wage and benefit growth.

Output and spending

  • BEA GDP GDP/GDI, expenditure contributions, final sales, prices, and revisions.
  • Personal income and outlays Income, consumption, saving, and PCE inflation.
  • Census retail trade Nominal goods spending; not a complete measure of consumption.
  • Federal Reserve G.17 Industrial production and capacity utilization.
  • Atlanta Fed GDPNow Mechanical current-quarter nowcast; not an official Atlanta Fed forecast.

Credit and financial conditions

  • Chicago Fed National Financial Conditions Index Weekly measures of U.S. financial conditions across money, debt, equity, and banking markets.
  • Federal Reserve H.8 Weekly bank loans, securities, deposits, and borrowings.
  • Senior Loan Officer Opinion Survey Bank-reported lending standards, terms, and demand.
  • Federal Reserve G.19 Revolving and nonrevolving consumer credit.
  • Delinquencies and charge-offs Loan performance by category at commercial banks.
  • Freddie Mac mortgage rates Primary mortgage-market borrowing rates.

Forecast and calibration

  • Summary of Economic Projections Conditional participant projections, not a staff forecast or Committee promise.
  • Survey of Professional Forecasters Consensus, dispersion, and probability forecasts.
  • Atlanta Fed Taylor Rule Utility Explore sensitivity to gaps, coefficients, rules, and neutral-rate assumptions.
  • New York Fed natural-rate estimates Model-dependent estimates that are uncertain and revised.
  • ALFRED Historical vintages for studying revisions and real-time information sets.

Release calendars

  • Federal Reserve Board calendar FOMC materials, Beige Book, minutes, speeches, and statistical releases.
  • BLS 2026 release calendar CPI, Employment Situation, JOLTS, ECI, productivity, and PPI.
  • BEA release schedule GDP, personal income and outlays, PCE prices, trade, and regional accounts.
  • Census economic-indicators calendar Retail sales, housing, durable goods, construction, and trade.

Before a chart enters the presentation

A visually polished chart can still be analytically wrong. Make sure to double check the following.

  • Authoritative source and exact series identifier are recorded.
  • Latest observation, vintage, units, frequency, and seasonal adjustment are correct.
  • Year-over-year, annualized, and level changes are not confused.
  • Axes, labels, and time windows support honest comparison.
