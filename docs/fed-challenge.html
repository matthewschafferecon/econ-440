College Fed Challenge

College Fed Challenge

Analyze current conditions, forecast the near-term outlook, assess material risks, and defend a coherent monetary-policy recommendation.

2026 overview National rubric Building the presentation Google Drive · EMU login
Competition deadline: October 5, 2026, at 5:00 p.m. EDT.

The presentation is limited to 15 minutes. No student, adviser, or school identification may appear. Up to five students may participate in the video and Q&A; alternates are permitted. The final video must be uploaded to YouTube as unlisted and submitted through the official form.

Fed Challenge presentation

Four parts, one argument

The strongest presentation is not four disconnected briefings. Each part should narrow the decision problem and prepare the next.

01 Evidence Current economic conditions
02 Outlook Near-term forecast
03 Uncertainty Significant risks
04 Decision Policy recommendation

Graded sequence

Assignments build toward the submission

Fed Challenge preparation accounts for 50% of the course grade. The sequence progresses from individual research and speaking to full-team policy analysis, rehearsal, and production.

Presenter selection is separate from grading.

Non-presenters can earn full credit through preparation, analysis, research checks, visual aids, source verification, timing, recording, quality control, and other documented team contributions.

Evaluation

The five judging categories

Course activities target the top band of the national judging rubric: accurate and sophisticated knowledge, insightful research, persuasive organization, direct answers under pressure, and integral roles for every team member.

Knowledge Fed, economy, and monetary policy
Questions Direct, poised, quick, persuasive
Presentation Logical, coherent, confident
Research Insightful, supported, authoritative

Team organization

Key topics

Prices and inflation

CPI and PCE, composition, wages, productivity, expectations, and supply pressure.

Labor market and inclusion

Payrolls, unemployment, participation, labor flows, earnings, and meaningful group outcomes.

Real activity and demand

GDP/GDI, consumption, investment, industrial production, housing, trade, and nowcasts.

Credit and financial conditions

Yields, spreads, banks, lending standards, consumer credit, and policy transmission.

Forecasts, risks, and policy

What are credible near-term forecasts for each of these economic indicators? What scenarios should policymakers be anticipating? Where do risks to the Fed's mandate arise? How does this all translate to a monetary policy decision?

October 5

Final Submission Checklist

  • Presentation is no longer than 15 minutes.
  • All claims are student-authored and linked to credible evidence.
  • No names, school name, colors, emblems, logos, or other identifiers appear in the submission video.
  • Every presenter plays a substantial and integral role.
  • Video is uploaded to YouTube as unlisted and plays through the submitted link.

Watch recent national winners

Treat these presentations as evidence to analyze, not templates to copy. Economic conditions, policy choices, and competition rules change. Focus on how each team turns current evidence into a coherent policy argument.

Common rubric strengths to watch for

  • Knowledge: economic theory and Fed mechanics are used to interpret evidence.
  • Research: each statistic has a purpose in the outlook, risk assessment, or policy decision.
  • Presentation: a clear decision problem organizes the briefing, with disciplined transitions and a specific recommendation.
  • Teamwork: speakers have distinct, substantial roles while using a shared framework and vocabulary.

2025 national winner

Pace University

Watch for

  • An opening frame—the “unbalanced dual mandate”—that gives the entire presentation a decision problem.
  • Forecast claims built from underlying components and leading indicators rather than headline data alone.
  • Policy analysis that distinguishes temporary supply pressures from demand-sensitive inflation and includes balance-sheet implementation.

2024 national winner

Princeton University

Watch for

  • A tight organizing question—how fast and how far to cut—that carries from the outlook through the final vote.
  • Meaningful disagreement among speakers, with competing interpretations tested against data and policy lags.
  • A recommendation that specifies the target range, balance-sheet policy, and communication strategy.

2023 national winner

Harvard College

Watch for

  • A deliberate progression from current conditions to forecasts and risks, followed by policy deliberation.
  • Attention to monetary-policy lags, financial conditions, and the unequal incidence of inflation.
  • A pause recommendation framed as risk management—not indecision—with clear conditions for reassessment.

2026 format

What happens after the Oct. 5th submission

November 2–4

Virtual regional Q&A

Three teams from each region are expected to advance to this round. Judges ask questions for up to 15 minutes. The submitted presentation may be displayed, but no new slides or data may be introduced.

November 20

In-person national final

Seven teams travel to Washington, D.C. Finalists may update the original presentation with new data and should bring printed copies for judges.
