College Fed Challenge

Policy lab

Analyze current conditions, forecast the near-term outlook, assess material risks, and defend a coherent monetary-policy recommendation.

2026 overview National rubric Student study guide
Competition deadline: October 5, 2026, at 5:00 p.m. EDT.

The presentation is limited to 15 minutes. No student, adviser, or school identification may appear. Up to five students may participate in the video and Q&A; alternates are permitted. The final video must be uploaded to YouTube as unlisted and submitted through the official form.

Graded sequence

Assignments build toward the submission

Fed Challenge preparation accounts for 50% of the course grade. The sequence progresses from individual research and speaking to full-team policy analysis, rehearsal, and production.

Presenter selection is separate from grading.

Non-presenters can earn full credit through preparation, analysis, research checks, visual aids, source verification, timing, recording, quality control, and other documented team contributions.

Evaluation

The five score categories

Course activities target the top band of the national rubric: accurate and sophisticated knowledge, insightful research, persuasive organization, direct answers under pressure, and integral roles for every team member.

Knowledge Fed, economy, and monetary policy
Questions Direct, poised, quick, persuasive
Presentation Logical, coherent, confident
Research Insightful, supported, authoritative
Teamwork Substantial roles and coordination

2026 format

What happens after submission

The regional and national stages have different rules for the presentation, so the team will preserve the submitted version and keep a separate controlled revision path.

November 2–4

Virtual regional Q&A

Judges ask questions for up to 15 minutes. The submitted presentation may be displayed, but no new slides or data may be introduced.

November 20

In-person national final

Seven teams travel to Washington, D.C. Finalists may update the original presentation with new data and should bring printed copies for judges.

Required content

Four parts, one argument

The strongest presentation is not four disconnected briefings. Each part should narrow the decision problem and prepare the next.

01 Evidence Current economic conditions
02 Outlook Near-term forecast
03 Uncertainty Significant risks
04 Decision Policy recommendation

Team organization

Research desks

Every student begins with a primary and secondary desk. Cross-desk roles rotate so source control, forecasting, counterargument, and integration do not concentrate in one person.

Prices and inflation

CPI and PCE, composition, wages, productivity, expectations, and supply pressure.

Labor market and inclusion

Payrolls, unemployment, participation, labor flows, earnings, and meaningful group outcomes.

Real activity and demand

GDP/GDI, consumption, investment, industrial production, housing, trade, and nowcasts.

Credit and financial conditions

Yields, spreads, banks, lending standards, consumer credit, and policy transmission.

Forecast, risks, and policy

Common assumptions, forecast reconciliation, scenarios, benchmarks, and decision framework.

Production system

Recurring artifacts

These internal tools make the students' reasoning inspectable and reproducible without prescribing the substantive conclusion.

Weekly

One-page economic brief

Bottom line, claims, charts, caveat, outlook implication, policy implication, and open question.

Continuous

Claim-evidence ledger

Claim, source, vintage, transformation, alternative interpretation, confidence, and owner.

Forecast cycle

Forecast table

Historical endpoint, horizon, central estimate, range, assumptions, and benchmarks.

Scenario cycle

Risk register

Transmission, probability, impact, signposts, invalidation, and policy relevance.

Decision cycle

Policy matrix

Preferred stance versus alternatives across the mandate, risks, lags, and implementation.

Quality control

Chart and source audit

Publisher, identifier, units, frequency, transformation, revision, reproducibility, and claim fit.

Question period

Four kinds of Q&A

Every practice round includes all four official categories. Respondents are selected randomly, assumptions may change mid-answer, and a second student adds only a distinct contribution.

01

Follow-up

Evidence, analysis, assumptions, or recommendations from the presentation.

02

Hypothetical

A changed data path, shock, or policy condition that tests the reaction function.

03

Macroeconomic theory

Phillips curve, neutral rate, expectations, rules, transmission, and uncertainty.

04

Federal Reserve

Structure, mandate, implementation, balance sheet, communication, and facilities.

October 5

Submission control

Analytical work ends before the technical deadline. The final review is limited to accuracy, timing, anonymity, authorship, playback, and submission integrity.

  • Presentation is no longer than 15 minutes.
  • All claims are student-authored and linked to authoritative evidence.
  • No names, school name, colors, emblems, logos, or other identifiers appear.
  • Every presenter plays a substantial and integral role.
  • Video is uploaded to YouTube as unlisted and plays through the submitted link.
  • Required title uses the assigned team number.
  • Submission confirmation and the exact data vintage are archived privately.
