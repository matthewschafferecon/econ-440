Course arc

From evidence to policy

The first phase builds research, presentation, and policy-analysis skills through the October 5 submission and an October 8 post-assessment. The advanced monetary-economics seminar begins October 13, with contingency meetings if the team advances.

01 Sept. 1–10 Diagnose, research, and present
02 Sept. 15–17 Build the mini-team briefing
03 Sept. 22–24 Recommend and defend policy
04 Sept. 29–Oct. 8 Rehearse, submit, and reflect

Working method

A research seminar with a deadline

Students learn the material by producing briefs, interrogating evidence, reconciling forecasts, challenging policy alternatives, and explaining conclusions under pressure.

Authoritative data

Start at the source

Course analysis begins with official data and documentation. FRED is a powerful access layer, but the original publisher and series definition remain part of every source log.

Student authorship

The team owns the argument.

The Federal Reserve requires original student work. AI may support generic course infrastructure and faculty-created instruction, but it may not write or rewrite competition analysis, forecasts, recommendations, slides, scripts, or Q&A responses.
