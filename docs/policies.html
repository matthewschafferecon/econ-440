Course governance

Policies and participation

A fair course distinguishes academic assessment, research contribution, competition eligibility, presenter selection, and student authorship.

Original work

Student authorship and AI

The Federal Reserve states that a presentation written in whole or in part by AI is not original student work and may be disqualified. The course applies that rule to the full competition production process.

Permitted

Faculty-created course infrastructure

  • Generic instructional explanations and examples
  • Schedules, logistics, assignment templates, and public resource guides
  • Website construction and maintenance
  • Generic exercises that are not incorporated into the submission
  • Faculty administrative support unrelated to competition content

Not permitted for competition work

AI-generated student submission content

  • Economic analysis or interpretation
  • Forecasts, scenarios, or risk assessments
  • Monetary-policy recommendations or supporting arguments
  • Slide text, charts selected by AI, narration, or scripts
  • Drafted, rewritten, or rehearsed Q&A responses
When uncertain, stop before using the tool.

Ask the instructor whether the activity creates or alters competition content. Convenience is not a defense if student authorship becomes unclear.

Roles

Enrollment, auditing, and competition

These categories overlap, but they are not interchangeable. Participation expectations and eligibility must be explicit before research roles are assigned.

Enrolled student
Completes all graded work and remains in ECON 440 for the entire semester.
Auditor
Participates in the Fed Challenge phase under the instructor's stated expectations but does not receive a course grade.
Team member
An eligible undergraduate listed on the official competition registration.
Alternate
An eligible registered team participant who may contribute but does not appear in the video, presentation, or Q&A while serving as an alternate.
Presenter
One of the maximum five eligible students who appears in the video and, if selected, the Q&A.

Team formation

Presenter selection principles

Competition-role selection is separate from grading. The process should be announced in advance and should recognize research and alternate roles.

  • Eligibility under the 2026 competition rules is confirmed.
  • Analytical command extends beyond a student's primary research desk.
  • Student can explain evidence accurately and respond under pressure.
  • Student contributes reliably to the team's research and revision process.
  • The five-person group demonstrates coordination rather than individual dominance.
  • Selection does not determine the student's course grade.

Assessment
