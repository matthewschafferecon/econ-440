Practice design

Three drill formats

Do not preassign questions to research desks.

45 seconds

Rapid round

Ten seconds to organize, 45 to answer, and 15 for a second student to add one distinct point.

Three questions

Deep round

One primary question and two follow-ups; require the student to identify one fact that could overturn the answer.

15 minutes

Team simulation

One common question plus presentation, hypothetical, theory, and Fed-institution prompts under competition timing.

Official type 1

Presentation follow-ups

Test the evidence, assumptions, forecast, risks, and recommendation actually presented.

  1. Decisive evidence

    Which single piece of evidence contributes most to your diagnosis, and why?

  2. Revision risk

    What evidence in your presentation is most vulnerable to revision?

  3. Measure choice

    Why did you use this inflation or labor measure instead of the main alternative?

  4. Cross-check

    Which indicator is least consistent with your baseline, and how do you reconcile it?

  5. Forecast difference

    What is the largest difference between your forecast and the SEP, and which assumption explains it?

  6. Risk boundary

    Why is your top risk not already part of the baseline?

  7. Policy error

    What observation would show that your recommendation was a mistake?

  8. Alternative

    Why is your recommendation better than waiting—or moving more aggressively?

  9. Distribution

    Which group bears the largest near-term cost of your recommendation?

  10. Balance sheet

    How does balance-sheet policy interact with your target-range recommendation?

Official type 2

Changed conditions

State what changes, what does not, and which mechanism links the surprise to policy.

  1. Labor supply

    Unemployment rises 0.4 percentage point because participation increases. What changes?

  2. Weak payrolls

    Payroll growth is much weaker for three months, but unemployment is unchanged. What changes?

  3. Inflation composition

    Core PCE rises for two months because of one volatile component. What changes?

  4. Expectations

    Short-horizon inflation expectations rise, while longer-horizon measures remain stable. What changes?

  5. Productivity

    Productivity growth is materially stronger than your baseline assumes. What changes?

  6. Term premium

    Long Treasury yields rise while the expected policy path is unchanged. What changes?

  7. Credit shock

    Spreads widen and equities fall without deterioration in hard data. What changes?

  8. Dollar

    The broad dollar appreciates sharply. What changes for inflation and activity?

  9. Data conflict

    GDP is strong, but income and employment measures are weak. What changes?

  10. Revision

    A major revision removes the turning point central to your argument. What changes?

Official type 3

Macroeconomic theory

Explain the mechanism, not merely the vocabulary.

  1. Non-neutrality

    What makes monetary policy neutral in the long run but powerful in the short run?

  2. Phillips curve

    Which Phillips-curve mechanism is relevant to your inflation forecast?

  3. Neutral rate

    What is the neutral real interest rate, and why is it difficult to observe?

  4. Restrictiveness

    What evidence supports calling the current stance restrictive?

  5. Policy rules

    When does a Taylor-type rule improve analysis, and when might strict adherence be undesirable?

  6. Credit channel

    How is the credit channel distinct from the interest-rate channel?

  7. Information effect

    How can an FOMC announcement reveal information about the outlook?

  8. Heterogeneity

    How do different marginal propensities to consume change transmission?

  9. Credibility

    What is time inconsistency, and why does credibility matter?

  10. Financial stability

    When can financial-stability concerns conflict with price stability?

Official type 4

Federal Reserve institutions

Connect institutional details to policy implementation and transmission.

  1. FOMC

    Who votes on the FOMC, and how do other Reserve Bank presidents participate?

  2. Implementation

    What happens operationally after the FOMC changes its target range?

  3. Administered rates

    Why can the effective federal funds rate differ from interest on reserve balances?

  4. ON RRP

    What role does the overnight reverse repo facility play?

  5. Reserves

    How can Treasury General Account movements affect reserve balances?

  6. Balance sheet

    What does quantitative tightening do to the Fed's assets and liabilities?

  7. Discount window

    What is its purpose, and why might banks hesitate to use it?

  8. SEP

    Is the median dot an FOMC commitment? Why or why not?

  9. Liquidity

    How is lender-of-last-resort policy different from monetary-policy easing?

  10. Independence

    What kind of independence does monetary policy need, and what accountability accompanies it?

Debrief

Score the reasoning

Use 0 for absent or materially weak, 1 for partial, and 2 for strong.

Directness
Answers the question in the first sentence.
Accuracy
Uses correct concepts, facts, magnitudes, and qualifications.
Mechanism
States the relevant causal or institutional chain.
Evidence
Uses decisive evidence without inventing precision.
Conditionality
Identifies what would change the conclusion.
Teamwork
Adds, hands off, or corrects without collision or contradiction.
Keep the debrief narrow.

Identify the most important correction, one stronger piece of evidence, and the next follow-up question.
