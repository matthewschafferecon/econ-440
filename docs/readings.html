Seminar readings and applications

Eight modules connect monetary theory and institutions to evidence, policy judgment, and a short analytical product.

Reading structure.

Core readings are the shared foundation. Extensions support deeper technical work and may become optional if the team advances in the College Fed Challenge.

Reading sequence

Core texts and student products

Final due dates will be added after the class meeting pattern is confirmed.

  1. Money creation and the credit channel

    McLeay, Radia, and Thomas, “Money Creation in the Modern Economy.” Product: a balance-sheet walkthrough separating accounting from causation.

  2. The ample-reserves operating framework

    Ihrig, Senyuz, and Weinbach, “Implementing Monetary Policy in an ‘Ample-Reserves’ Regime.” Product: a memo tracing an FOMC decision into overnight rates.

  3. Transmission and identification

    Bernanke and Gertler, “Inside the Black Box.” Extension: Nakamura and Steinsson on high-frequency identification. Product: an FOMC event-study identification brief.

  4. Rules, discretion, and credibility

    Bernanke, “The Taylor Rule: A Benchmark for Monetary Policy?” paired with the Fed’s policy principles. Product: a rule-versus-discretion memo.

  5. Financial stability and lender of last resort

    Domanski, Moessner, and Nelson, “Central Banks as Lender of Last Resort.” Product: a facility-design memo with safeguards.

  6. Heterogeneity and distribution

    Kaplan and Violante, “Microeconomic Heterogeneity and Macroeconomic Shocks.” Extension: Auclert on the redistribution channel. Product: a household-exposure matrix.

  7. International spillovers and the global dollar

    Gopinath and coauthors, “Dominant Currency Paradigm.” Extension: Rey on international transmission. Product: a two-country spillover map.

  8. Monetary-fiscal interactions and independence

    Nelson, “The Practice of U.S. Monetary Policy Independence.” Product: a governance memo distinguishing forms of independence.

Before class

The one-page analytical note

Analyze the argument; do not merely summarize the abstract.

Question
What uncertainty or disagreement is the reading designed to resolve?
Mechanism
What behavior, friction, or institution generates the result?
Evidence
What data or model discipline the claim?
Assumption
Which assumption is doing the most work?
Challenge
What is the strongest objection or alternative interpretation?
Policy
What would a policymaker do differently if the result is correct?
