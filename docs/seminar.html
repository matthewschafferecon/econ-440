Part II

Advanced monetary economics

The seminar begins October 13 and examines advanced monetary-policy tools, transmission, policy design, monetary theory, international arrangements, and digital money.

Tuesdays and Thursdays · 12:30–1:45 p.m. · October 13–December 10

If the team advances.

If EMU advances, November 3 may become a regional Q&A preparation meeting and November 19 may become a national-final preparation meeting. Seminar material will be compressed, reassigned, or moved, and the seminar’s 50% course weight may be reduced.

Seminar sequence

Topics and dates

Readings, discussion questions, and quiz details will be posted on Canvas.

  1. October 13 & 15 · Quantitative easing and balance-sheet policy

    Large-scale asset purchases, central-bank balance sheets, portfolio-balance effects, signaling, market functioning, and policy normalization.

  2. October 27 & 29 · Forward guidance

    Expectations, commitment, communication, credibility, state contingency, and the limits of promises about future policy.

  3. November 3 & 5 · Monetary transmission

    Interest rates, banks, credit, balance sheets, asset prices, exchange rates, expectations, heterogeneity, and policy lags.

  4. November 10 & 12 · Rules versus discretion

    Policy rules, commitment, time inconsistency, credibility, judgment, uncertainty, and institutional design.

  5. November 17 & 19 · Does money still matter?

    Money demand, monetary aggregates, liquidity, credit, operating frameworks, and whether money retains explanatory or policy value.

  6. November 24 · Endogenous business-cycle theory and money

    Internal propagation, monetary mechanisms, amplification, and the interpretation of cyclical fluctuations. No class November 26.

  7. December 1 & 3 · The international monetary system

    Exchange-rate regimes, global liquidity, the international role of the dollar, spillovers, policy coordination, and external constraints.

  8. December 8 & 10 · Digital currencies and stablecoins

    Central-bank digital currency, stablecoin design, payments, financial intermediation, monetary sovereignty, regulation, and policy transmission.

Seminar method

How to discuss each reading

Students should move between theory, institutions, and evidence without treating a reading’s abstract as a substitute for analysis.

Question
What uncertainty or disagreement is the material designed to resolve?
Mechanism
Which behavior, friction, institution, or equilibrium condition produces the result?
Evidence
What data or model discipline the claim?
Assumption
Which assumption is doing the most work?
Challenge
What is the strongest objection or alternative interpretation?
Policy
What would a policymaker do differently if the argument is correct?
