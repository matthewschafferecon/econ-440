Analytical spine

Four questions for every issue

A list of indicators is not an economic diagnosis.

01 State What do the most relevant data say now?
02 Outlook Where is the variable likely to move?
03 Mechanism Why should it move, and how would policy affect it?
04 Decision What does this imply for monetary policy?

Foundation 1

The Fed and policy implementation

Distinguish the FOMC decision, the operating framework, observed market rates, and the broader stance of policy.

Decision
The FOMC chooses the target range and communicates its policy stance.
Implementation
Administered rates and Desk operations keep overnight rates within the target range in an ample-reserves framework.
Transmission
Expected policy, yields, credit, asset prices, exchange rates, and balance sheets influence demand, employment, and inflation.
Communication
The statement, implementation note, SEP, minutes, and press conference serve different purposes.

Foundation 2

Reading real-time data

Record enough metadata that another analyst can reproduce the result.

  • Exact publisher, release, series name, and identifier
  • Units, frequency, seasonal adjustment, date range, and transformation
  • Release date, retrieval date, and revision status
  • A comparison that makes the observation economically meaningful
  • A clear separation among observed data, forecasts, assumptions, and judgments
Common error.

A percent change is not a percentage-point change. Do not annualize a series already expressed at an annual rate, and check revisions before declaring a turning point.

Foundation 3

Diagnosing current conditions

Use corroborating evidence and make disagreements across indicators explicit.

Prices

Inflation

Compare CPI and PCE, headline and core, recent momentum and 12-month rates, breadth, wages, productivity, expectations, and supply effects.

Employment

Labor markets

Reconcile payrolls, unemployment, participation, flows, vacancies, quits, hours, wages, revisions, and group outcomes.

Demand and supply

Economic activity

Separate levels from growth and compare GDP, GDI, final sales, consumption, investment, housing, production, and potential supply.

Transmission

Credit and financial conditions

Distinguish price from quantity, supply from demand, risk-free rates from premia, and financial tightening from financial stress.

Foundation 4

Forecasts, risks, and scenarios

A policy forecast is a transparent conditional judgment, not a set of isolated point guesses.

Baseline

Define assumptions

Horizon, policy path, financial conditions, fiscal setting, external conditions, central paths, and ranges.

Consistency

Reconcile variables

Output and employment, costs and inflation, consumption and income, credit and the assumed policy path.

Benchmarks

Explain differences

Compare with the SEP, surveys, market paths, and nowcasts without treating any benchmark as an answer key.

Risk

Specify signposts

Trigger, causal chain, horizon, probability, impact, observable signposts, invalidation, and policy implication.

Foundation 5

A complete policy recommendation

The recommendation should follow from the diagnosis, forecast, and risk assessment.

  • State the target-range decision and any relevant balance-sheet or implementation choice.
  • Identify the diagnosis and forecast that support the decision.
  • Compare the recommendation with serious alternatives.
  • Explain how uncertainty and asymmetric risks affect the choice.
  • State what future evidence would cause the team to change course.
  • Address material distributional and financial-stability implications.

Oral defense

Answer, reason, evidence, condition

This is a reasoning structure, not a memorized script.

01 Answer Give the conclusion first.
02 Reason State the central mechanism.
03 Evidence Use one or two decisive facts.
04 Condition Say what would change the view.

Self-assessment

Presenter mastery check

Every presenter should be able to do these without slides.

  • Explain the dual mandate, FOMC role, and ample-reserves implementation.
  • State the team's inflation, labor, activity, and credit diagnoses.
  • Reconstruct the forecast, assumptions, benchmarks, and uncertainty.
  • Explain the top risks, causal chains, signposts, and invalidation conditions.
  • Defend the recommendation against at least two alternatives.
  • Interpret every chart and identify its source and transformation.
  • Respond to an important counterfactual data surprise.
  • Explain material effects across groups and sectors.
  • Identify the evidence that would change the team's view.
