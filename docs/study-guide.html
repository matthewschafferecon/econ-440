Foundation 2

Reading real-time data

  • Note the publisher, release, series name, and identifier.
  • Know what units data are in, the frequency of observation, whether a seasonal adjustment has been performed, and whether the data has undergone a transformation.
  • Check the release date, retrieval date, and revision status to make sure you have the most up-to-date data.
  • Choose sample periods (x-axis) and units (y-axis) that make the data economically meaningful.
Common error.

A percent change is not a percentage-point change. A percent change is the growth rate in a level variable—for example, if GDP is $1,000 and grows by 2%, that means GDP increases to $1,020. A percentage-point change is a change in a growth rate—for example, if GDP growth was 3% last quarter, a 2 percentage-point increase means GDP grew by 5% this quarter. A basis point is one-hundredth of a percentage point—for example, a 0.25 percentage-point increase can also be described as a 25 basis point increase.

Step 1

Diagnosing current conditions

Prices

Inflation

Compare CPI and PCE, headline and core, recent momentum and 12-month rates, breadth, wages, productivity, expectations, and supply effects.

Employment

Labor markets

Reconcile payrolls, unemployment, participation, flows, vacancies, quits, hours, wages, revisions, and group outcomes.

Demand and supply

Economic activity

Separate levels from growth and compare GDP, GDI, final sales, consumption, investment, housing, production, and potential supply.

Transmission

Credit and financial conditions

Distinguish price from quantity, supply from demand, risk-free rates from premia, and financial tightening from financial stress.

Step 2

Forecasts, risks, and scenarios

Baseline

Define assumptions

State what the forecast assumes about monetary policy, financial conditions, fiscal policy, and the global economy. Make the forecast horizon clear.

Consistency

Reconcile variables

Make sure that forecasts for different indicators are consistent. It would be odd if output is expected to grow but consumption is expected to shrink!

Benchmarks

Explain differences

Compare the team's forecasts with the SEP, professional surveys, market expectations, and nowcasts. If the team disagrees, explain why.

Risk

Specify signposts

For each important risk, explain how it would affect the economy, what data would signal that it is occurring, and how it would change the policy recommendation.

Step 3

A complete policy recommendation

  • State the target-range decision and any relevant balance-sheet or implementation choice.
  • Identify the diagnosis and forecast that support the decision.
  • Compare the recommendation with serious alternatives.
  • Explain how uncertainty and asymmetric risks affect the choice.
  • State what future evidence would cause the team to change course.
  • Address material distributional and financial-stability implications.

Oral defense

Answer, reason, evidence, condition

01 Answer Give the conclusion first.
02 Reason State the central mechanism.
03 Evidence Use one or two decisive facts.
04 Condition Say what would change the view.

Self-assessment

Presentation checklist

  • Explain the dual mandate and FOMC role.
  • State the team's inflation, labor, activity, and credit diagnoses.
  • Explain how the team arrived at its forecasts.
  • Explain the top risks to the Fed's mandate.
  • Defend the policy recommendation against reasonable alternatives.
  • Interpret charts and identify sources.
  • Identify the evidence that would change the team's view.
